Transcribe anywhere, even in airplane mode

Murmur needs the internet once, to download its speech model. After that, recording, transcription, search, editing and export all work with no connection at all.

Can I transcribe audio without an internet connection?

Yes. Murmur downloads its Whisper speech model one time over Wi-Fi. From then on, transcription runs entirely on your iPhone, iPad or Mac, so it works in airplane mode, on a plane, underground or in a building with no signal. Nothing is sent anywhere while you work.

In the app

How it works

Install and open Murmur

Finish the short onboarding and allow the microphone.

Download the speech model on Wi-Fi

Choose Large v3 Turbo or Small. This happens once.

Go offline

Switch on airplane mode and record. The transcript appears when you stop.

Details

What you get

No signal, no problem

Lecture halls, courtrooms, hospitals, field sites and flights often have poor or no connection. Murmur does not care.

Nothing to wait on

There is no upload queue and no server load. A transcript starts as soon as you stop recording.

No data plan used

Long recordings are big files. Because nothing is uploaded, transcribing an hour of audio uses zero mobile data.

Wi-Fi-only download

The one-time model download waits for Wi-Fi by default, so it never eats into your plan.

Summaries offline too

On devices with Apple Intelligence, meeting and lecture summaries are made on the device as well.

Sync is optional

iCloud sync is the only feature that uses the network, and it is off until you turn it on.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Do I need internet to use Murmur?

Only once, to download the speech model. Recording, transcription, search, editing and export all work offline after that.

Does Murmur work on a plane?

Yes. Once the model is downloaded you can record and transcribe in airplane mode.

What still needs a connection?

Buying or restoring Murmur Pro goes through the App Store, and optional iCloud sync needs a connection to copy notes between your devices. Transcription itself never does.
